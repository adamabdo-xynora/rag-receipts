## What Counts as a Claim

A claim covers goods that arrived damaged, goods that arrived but were not ordered, and lines that were invoiced but not delivered. A claim is not a return: a return concerns goods the customer no longer wants, while a claim concerns goods that did not arrive in the condition or quantity we invoiced. The two follow different windows and different approval paths, and filing one does not preserve the other.

## Claim Windows

Damage claims must be lodged within three working days of delivery. Short shipment claims must be lodged within one working day of delivery, because pick records and route load sheets are reconciled overnight and a shortfall is far harder to trace once that reconciliation has run. Both windows run from the delivery date on the packing slip.

## Evidence We Require

Every damage claim needs a photograph of the outer carton showing the delivery label, a photograph of the damage itself, and the mill lot or carton code where one is printed. Short shipment claims need a photograph of the packing slip with the missing lines marked. Claims arrive at claims@example.com or 555-0173; the ordering desk cannot accept them, because that queue strips attachments.

## Collection of Rejected Goods

Goods rejected under a claim are collected on the account's next scheduled delivery run at no charge. Rejected goods must be left exactly as they were delivered, in the carton they arrived in, and must not be repacked, consolidated, or palletised by the customer, because repacking destroys the evidence the claim rests on. Goods are collected from the same access point used for delivery.

## Outcome and Timing

An assessed claim results in a replacement on the next run for that zone, or a credit note against the original invoice, at the customer's election. Assessment is completed within two working days of receiving complete evidence. Incomplete evidence pauses the clock rather than voiding the claim, and the customer is told exactly what is missing.
